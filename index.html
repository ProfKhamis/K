Higher/Lower

Fires a Higher + Lower pair on the same tick, repeated back-to-back the number of times set below — same batching pattern as Bulk Over/Under. Per Deriv's Higher/Lower contract: Higher (contract type CALL) pays out if the exit spot is strictly above its barrier; Lower (contract type PUT) pays out if the exit spot is strictly below its barrier. A tie on either barrier loses that leg. Barrier Offset sets both barriers symmetrically around the entry spot — e.g. an offset of 0.5 buys Higher at entry+0.5 and Lower at entry−0.5 at the same time. Honest caveat: the two barriers create a dead zone between them — if the exit spot lands inside that zone, both legs lose. This doesn't change the underlying odds; it's a fixed pair-entry rule, not a prediction.

Accumulator

Different mechanic from everything else here: no fixed duration. Your stake compounds by the growth rate you pick for every tick the price stays inside Deriv's range for that rate; the instant price breaks the range, the contract is knocked out and the stake is lost. Set a take profit and Deriv closes the contract automatically the moment it's reached. Click Run and it keeps buying a fresh Accumulator every time one settles — win, loss, or take-profit — until today's session target is hit or you hit Stop (which also force-sells any position still open).

Requires the Session Tracker to be running — it's what tells this strategy when to stop. Higher growth rates compound faster but use a narrower price range, so they get knocked out sooner on average.

AI Over 2/Under 7

DBot-style pattern trigger, not a rolling-window average like AI Over 1/Under 8 above. It watches the last 3 digits: for Over 2 it arms when two "lower" digits (0, 1, or 2 — 2 counts as lower since Over 2 loses on a tie) are immediately followed by one "upper" digit (3–9); for Under 7 it arms when two "upper" digits (7, 8, or 9 — 7 counts as upper since Under 7 loses on a tie) are immediately followed by one confirming lower digit that is not 0 or 1 (so 2–6). Once armed it fires on whichever tick lands next — no re-checking, exactly like the Auto Over/Under DBot strategy. Honest caveat: each tick's digit is drawn independently, so this pattern doesn't change the odds of the next digit — it's a fixed entry rule, not a prediction.
